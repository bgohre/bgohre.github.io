Opening note

November, in the middle of the work

November is Milestones season. The midyear reporting window opened October 26 and runs through January 8, 2027. The work that fills this month is mostly invisible: chasing down evaluations, checking rosters, and sitting in committee meetings where a semester of work becomes a judgment about a trainee.

This issue stays close to that work. Hot topics covers what changed this month and why it matters to your office. Accreditation corner takes the Clinical Competency Committee apart, requirement by requirement, and shows what it looks like in practice. Wellness is a short gratitude lap for the holiday season, because the people doing the work deserve a pause before the year turns.

One more thing before we start. Danielle and Nicole recorded a holiday episode of Coast to Coast GME this week, called "Gratitude in the Holiday Season," about navigating the holidays while supporting trainees and pausing to thank the people who keep patient care going, in the clinic and behind the scenes. Hot topics

What changed this month

Milestones season is open

The midyear Milestones reporting window opened October 26, 2026 and runs through January 8, 2027. Milestones assessments can be submitted through ADS at any point in the window.

Two scheduling changes are worth knowing. Residents and fellows who started off cycle are now scheduled for a Milestones evaluation once they have been in the program for at least 60 days. Residents and fellows who are on a leave of absence, or recently returned from one, are also scheduled. Both changes apply to this window and every window after it.

Why it matters: Your roster may include people you did not expect. Check it before the committee meets, not after.

Source: ACGME Milestones overview · ACGME e-Communication, Oct. 26, 2026

The Institutional Requirements revision has a date

At its September meeting, the ACGME Board of Directors approved a major revision of the Institutional Requirements. The revised requirements take effect July 1, 2027, and the new text is already posted on the ACGME website.

This is an approved revision, not a proposal. The Common Program Requirements rewrite from September is still out for comment, and the two should not be confused. One is coming. The other is still being shaped.

Why it matters: July 1, 2027 gives your institution time to compare current practice against the new text and update policies before the effective date. Start with the sections your office touches every day.

Sources: ACGME e-Communication, Oct. 12, 2026 · Institutional Requirements, effective July 1, 2027 (PDF)

ACGME 2027 is three months out

The 2027 Annual Educational Conference runs February 25 to 27 in San Antonio, Texas, at the Henry B. González Convention Center. This year’s theme is "Meaning in Medicine." Registration is open, and Early Bird pricing ends December 16, 2026.

I will be there, co-presenting a session on redesigning GME administrator onboarding with Amy Rosa and Beth Smith. The session is called "From Survival Mode to Sustainable Practice." If you are coming, I hope we get to say hello in person.

Why it matters: The December 16 Early Bird deadline is the date to watch. Hotel reservations open for registered attendees, so registering early makes the rest of the trip easier to plan.

Source: ACGME Annual Educational Conference

SITE registration opens in November

Registration for the Subspecialty In-Training Exam opens this month. Confirm your fellow roster now, so registration is a quick task when it opens instead of a scramble later.

Why it matters: The roster is the work. Once it is right, the rest takes minutes.

Your old ADS reports are gone

Reports more than five years old left ADS on October 31, 2026. Resident and Fellow Survey, Faculty Survey, and Milestones PDF reports from before that cutoff are no longer available in the system.

If you downloaded yours in October, they are in your files now. If a self-study or APE file pointed at an older report, check that the copy you saved is the one you need.

Why it matters: The history is no longer retrievable from ADS. What you saved is what you have.

Source: ACGME e-Communication, Oct. 26, 2026

Accreditation corner

Milestones season and the quiet work of the CCC

November is Milestones season. The midyear reporting window opened October 26 and runs through January 8, 2027. That window is when a semester of evaluation work becomes a Milestones report, and the Clinical Competency Committee is the group that turns evaluations into judgments.

What the requirement asks

The program director appoints the Clinical Competency Committee. At a minimum, it has three members of the program faculty, and the program keeps a written description of the committee’s responsibilities. The committee’s job has three parts: review all resident or fellow evaluations semiannually; prepare and ensure the reporting of Milestones evaluations for each trainee semiannually to the ACGME; and advise the program director on each trainee’s progress, including promotion, remediation, and dismissal.

Note the shape of that job. The CCC does not generate the evaluations. It reads them, weighs them, and makes a Milestones judgment the program director can stand behind. That distinction matters in November, because the meeting is only as good as the packet that goes into it.

The requirements set the shape. How your office runs the meeting, builds the packet, and records the decision is practice, and your office gets to choose it. The ideas below are recommended, not required.

What this could look like in practice

  • Verify the roster first. Confirm every trainee who should be reported is on the ADS roster, and handle anyone who left or changed programs before the meeting, not during it.
  • Chase missing evaluations before the meeting. A committee cannot review what is not there, and a late evaluation found during the meeting slows every decision after it.
  • Build the packet around the trainee, not the rotation. One trainee’s evaluations, Milestones history, and prior committee notes together, so the committee judges a trajectory, not a stack of forms.
  • Meet before the semiannual evaluations go out. The committee advises the program director, and that advice should shape the evaluation conversation, not follow it.
  • Record the decision, not just the rating. A Milestones level plus one line on why the committee landed there is what makes the next cycle’s conversation possible.

This month’s checklist

  • Confirm the CCC meeting date sits before your semiannual evaluation meetings.
  • Before the meeting, verify the ADS roster and close the gaps in rotation evaluations.
  • Ahead of January 8, set your internal submission date and assign who enters and who approves the Milestones data.

Sources: ACGME Milestones overview (midyear window October 26, 2026 through January 8, 2027) · Common Program Requirements, Evaluation section (Clinical Competency Committee)

Accreditation corner offers an educational perspective, not official ACGME guidance. Always verify against the current requirements on acgme.org and check with your DIO or institutional GME office.

Wellness

A gratitude lap

October’s issue ended with a recovery lap, and November is the right month to keep moving slowly. The year is almost done. The holidays are here. The calendar will not announce a pause, so this one is yours to make.

Gratitude is the simplest version of that pause. Think of three people whose work made yours possible this year. A faculty member who wrote the evaluation on time. A colleague who covered a call. A trainee who trusted you with a hard conversation. Then tell one of them. A short note, a few honest sentences, is enough. People remember being thanked far longer than they remember the meeting where it happened.

Danielle and Nicole talked about this on the holiday episode of Coast to Coast GME, and they said it better than I can: the work of caring for patients runs on people, in the clinic and behind the scenes, and naming that out loud matters. If you have seven minutes, it is worth a listen on the podcast page.

Gratitude is not extra work. It is the work, said out loud.

One more thing. The holidays ask a lot of GME offices, and the ask does not stop at the office door. Decide in advance which evening events you will attend and which you will not. A boundary set in November is much easier to keep than one invented in December.

If you’re carrying more than ordinary stress, please reach out to someone you trust or to your employee assistance program. In the U.S., you can call or text 988 to reach the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline at any time.

Until next month

Thanks for reading, and for the work you do that most people never see.
